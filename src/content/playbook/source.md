# TTI Recruiting — Recruiter Script & Scenario Playbook

Version: September 30, 2026 · Language: US English

## Purpose and use

Help a beginner recruiter lead a short, confident conversation from first contact to a relevant application. Sound relaxed, direct, and capable. Ask one question, listen, and move to the next useful step.

This is a broad scenario bank, not a claim that every possible conversation can be predicted. For an unfamiliar situation, use the fallback in Section 18. Read only the branch that fits; never read this whole document to a driver.

“98% closing” describes the desired confidence, not a measured result. The goal is qualified applications and informed decisions; an application is not a hire or a guaranteed offer. All spoken lines below are original drafts.

## 1. Delivery and verified offer details

- Speak with calm energy. Be quick between steps, not rushed while the driver answers.
- Ask one question at a time. Do not interrupt an answer to complete a checklist.
- Reflect the driver's priority before pitching: “You want [priority]. Here is how this option lines up.”
- End each useful branch with one question or one agreed action.
- After the close, pause. Let the driver answer.
- A clear refusal ends the pitch. A request to stop contact ends outreach.
- Use the actual recruiter identity, callback number, and configured referral link. Do not invent a referral URL or claim the driver applied to TTI without a verified record.
- Promise your next action only when you can carry it out. Verify uncertain details instead of improvising.

### Offer fields to verify before quoting

| Field | What must be known |
|---|---|
| Company and opening | Actual carrier, role, hiring area, current availability |
| Employment type | W-2 or 1099, as documented for this opening |
| Pay | Amount/range, pay basis, practical assumptions, guarantee conditions if any |
| Mileage or percentage | Paid-mile method or revenue definition; accessorials and exclusions |
| Deductions and costs | Documented deductions, deposits, escrow, relevant charges |
| Home time | Frequency, time at home, eligibility and operating conditions |
| Routes | Local/regional/OTR, freight, territory, schedule |
| Equipment | Documented truck, transmission, equipment policies |
| Benefits and bonuses | Eligibility, waiting period, payout terms |
| Screening | Current carrier criteria and review process |
| Orientation | Location, timing, travel, lodging, pay, reimbursement terms |
| Application | Correct link, recruiter attribution, required next step |

If a field is unknown: **“Let me verify that so you get the actual terms. I’ll follow up by [agreed time/channel].”**

## 2. Main call script

### Open

“Hey [Name], it’s [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting. I help CDL drivers find their next company. You got a minute?”

### Find the reason

“What would make a move worth it for you—better pay, more home time, or different routes?”

Let them answer.

“Got it. What’s the one thing your next company has to get right?”

### Choose the path

“Are you looking for W-2 or 1099?”

**W-2:** Use the external application branch in Section 5. Skip the internal 1099 question sequence.

**1099:** “Let’s check what fits. What ZIP are you based in?” Then ask the required qualification questions one at a time.

### Present a verified match

“Based on what you told me, [Company] looks like the strongest fit: [verified pay and basis], [home time], and [routes/equipment]. That gets you [their priority]. How does that line up with what you want?”

If two options fit:

“[Company A] gives you [benefit and tradeoff]. [Company B] gives you [benefit and tradeoff]. Which fits you better?”

### Close

“Good. Let’s get your application started for [Company]. That gets the review moving; you decide whether to accept an offer. Ready?”

If the next step is a link:

“Can I text you the link? If you’re parked and ready, I can stay with you while you open it.”

### Confirm the next step

“We’re moving forward with [Company/application path]. Next is [step]. I’ll [specific action] by [time]. What’s the best way to reach you?”

The spoken opening and pitch should be short. Total call length depends on the driver's questions and required qualification; never skip required checks just to shorten the call.

## 3. Contact and opening scenarios

### S001 — Driver answers and is available
**Say:** “Perfect. What would make a new company worth your time?”
**Next:** Identify the main priority, then employment preference.

### S002 — “Who is this?”

**Say:** “This is [Recruiter], a driver recruiter with TTI Recruiting. I'm reaching out about CDL-A job options. Is now a good time?”

**Next:** Answer the identity question directly. State the company and purpose before an offer or link. Never use 'the guy who will change your life' as the introduction.

### S003 — “Where did you get my number?”

**Say:** “The contact source shown in our record is [verified contact source]. I can tell you what is recorded there. Would you like to continue, or stop here?”

**Next:** Render the source only when verified. Never claim an application, prior consent or previous conversation without evidence.

### S004 — “I never applied to you.”
**Say:** “Understood. I won’t tell you that you did. I’ll check the source. Would you like to hear why I called, or would you prefer we stop here?”
**Next:** Respect the answer and record any contact preference.

### S005 — Wrong number
**Say:** “Thanks for letting me know. I’ll mark this as a wrong number. Have a good one.”
**Next:** Remove it from active outreach according to the contact workflow; preserve the database record appropriately.

### S006 — Someone else answers
**Say:** “Hi, may I speak with [Name]? This is [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting.”
**Next:** If unavailable, offer your callback number. Do not disclose application, pay, or screening details to another person.

### S007 — Busy or unavailable

**Say:** “No problem. What time should I call you back, and what time zone will you be in?”

**Next:** Stop the offer pitch. Ask only what is needed for the callback. If they cannot choose a time, let them call the verified line when ready; do not invent an agreed appointment.

### S008 — Driver has only 30 seconds
**Say:** “Quick version: I’m calling about CDL opportunities. Are you looking for W-2 or 1099, and when can we talk properly?”
**Next:** Get the preferred path and callback; do not squeeze a full screening into 30 seconds.

### S009 — Busy, vague callback request

**Say:** “Sure. What time tomorrow works, and is that your local time in [known location]?”

**Next:** Use the driver's time zone and confirm the date. 'Tomorrow morning' is a window, not a precise appointment. If they prefer a window, record it as a window and arrange a realistic follow-up.

### S010 — Poor signal or noisy truck
**Say:** “I’m losing a few words. Let’s reconnect when you’ve got a clearer line. What time works?”
**Next:** Confirm contact details only if heard clearly; never guess answers.

### S011 — Driver sounds annoyed by recruiter calls
**Say:** “I hear you. I’ll keep it simple: are you considering a move, or should I leave it here?”
**Next:** If interested, ask their one priority. If not, end respectfully.

### S012 — “Not interested.”
**Say:** “Understood. Thanks for your time.”
**Next:** Record declined. Do not turn a clear refusal into another objection cycle.

### S013 — “Stop calling me.”

**Say:** “Understood. I'll mark this number Do Not Contact. Take care.”

**Next:** Use the DNC control immediately. Do not send a final offer, link, referral pitch or another persuasion message. Preserve the suppression when the call is finished.

### S014 — Driver calls back after voicemail
**Say:** “Glad we connected. I called about CDL opportunities. What would you want to improve in your next job?”
**Next:** Start discovery; do not repeat the entire voicemail.

### S015 — Driver believes you are their current carrier
**Say:** “Just to be clear, I’m with TTI Recruiting. I’m calling about exploring another company.”
**Next:** Correct the misunderstanding before collecting information.

### S016 — Driver requests another language
**Say:** “Which language works best for you? Let me check whether we can arrange someone who can help.”
**Next:** Use an available language resource or schedule an appropriate handoff; do not pretend to understand.

## 4. Motivation and timing

### S017 — “I already have a job.”

**Say:** “Good to hear. Is there anything you'd want to improve, or are you happy to stay where you are?”

**Next:** Respect a satisfied driver. If they choose to compare, ask what would make a move worthwhile. If nothing needs to change, finish politely. Do not tell them their current employer is bad.

### S018 — Happy at current company
**Say:** “Sounds like you’ve got a good setup. Want to leave it here, or would you like to hear about a specific improvement?”
**Next:** Continue only if invited.

### S019 — Wants more money

**Say:** “For [verified company and role], the confirmed starting rate is [verified rate and paid-mile or revenue basis]. The schedule is [verified schedule]. What rate or weekly amount would make a move worthwhile for you?”

**Next:** If a figure is not verified, use the fallback. Distinguish solo from team and base pay from conditional bonuses. A higher CPM does not establish higher take-home pay. State any remaining review condition.

### S020 — Wants more home time

**Say:** “How many days out would work for you, and how many days do you need at home? I'll compare that with the company's actual schedule.”

**Next:** Do not promise a pass through home, every weekend off, or parking near home unless the carrier confirms it. If the driver needs 14 days out and the offer requires 21, mark the conflict.

### S021 — Wants predictable work
**Say:** “When you say predictable, do you mean a steady schedule, steady miles, or both?”
**Next:** Verify the relevant schedule and workload details.

### S022 — Angry with dispatcher/current carrier
**Say:** “Sounds frustrating. What needs to be different at the next company?”
**Next:** Extract the requirement; avoid joining in personal attacks.

### S023 — Ready to start immediately
**Say:** “Let’s check the fit and the earliest confirmed start. What’s your actual availability?”
**Next:** Verify orientation and review timing; never promise immediate placement.

### S024 — Wants to start weeks later
**Say:** “Good to know. What date are you targeting?”
**Next:** Match current possibilities and agree when to recheck availability.

### S025 — Just browsing
**Say:** “That’s fine. What would have to be better for you to seriously consider moving?”
**Next:** Share one relevant verified option; do not force an application.

### S026 — Unsure what they want
**Say:** “Let’s check the practical details against the current offers.”
**Next:** Return to the next unanswered practical qualification question. Record volunteered priorities as optional notes; no separate motivation question.

### S027 — Wants everything with no tradeoffs
**Say:** “I’ll keep that requirement in the comparison.”
**Next:** Retain already stated requirements in optional notes and matching. Return to the next unanswered practical question; no separate deal-breaker question.

### S028 — Needs family approval
**Say:** “Makes sense. What details will your family want to review?”
**Next:** Send a verified summary and agree on a follow-up after that conversation.

## 5. W-2 and 1099 routing

**TTI workflow direction:** W-2 drivers use the external W-2 application at `https://apply.ttirecruiting.us`, through the actual configured recruiter referral link. Skip the remaining internal 1099 questions and validation. For 1099, preserve all 14 stored fields and the existing eligibility validation. Priority and deal-breakers are optional record-only fields; do not ask a motivation interview or require them to continue. This document does not establish the exact app fields or replace the existing approved questionnaire.

### S029 — Driver chooses W-2

**Say:** “For W-2, the next step is our driver application. It gives us the details we need to check options that fit you. Is it okay if I text you your application link?”

**Next:** Use the assigned referral link for this recruiter. Ask only a brief priority question if the driver raises a specific requirement; do not run the 1099 questionnaire. The form does not guarantee a job or a specific company. Use a callback time the recruiter can meet. Do not quote a 10-minute response promise unless that service is staffed and receipt tracking supports it.

### S030 — Driver chooses 1099

**Say:** “Would you like me to move forward with your application for [Company]?”

**Next:** Pause for a real yes. Do not send an external apply link on this path. Check any required application fields and the document checklist. An agreement to provide documents is still a pending application. Use the explicit Submit Application action. Finish Conversation saves the call outcome and summary; it does not submit the driver application.

### S031 — Driver does not understand the distinction

**Say:** “We can check both types where the carrier offers them. Which path would you like to start with?”

**Next:** Read the carrier's actual classification and terms. Do not promise benefits or give personal tax advice. If the driver needs an explanation, have a senior recruiter explain the verified offer. Lease and owner-operator work continue through the internal path.

### S032 — Open to either

**Say:** “We can check both types where the carrier offers them. Which path would you like to start with?”

**Next:** Read the carrier's actual classification and terms. Do not promise benefits or give personal tax advice. If the driver needs an explanation, have a senior recruiter explain the verified offer. Lease and owner-operator work continue through the internal path.

### S033 — Changes from 1099 to W-2
**Say:** “No problem. We’ll switch to the W-2 application path. Can I send you the link?”
**Next:** Stop the remaining 1099 sequence; update preference and preserve already recorded history.

### S034 — Changes from W-2 to 1099
**Say:** “Got it. For 1099, we’ll go through the company-matching questions together.”
**Next:** Resume the approved internal sequence; do not assume an external application satisfies internal validation.

### S035 — Wants lease purchase or owner-operator work
**Say:** “Are you looking to bring your own truck, lease a truck, or drive a company truck?”
**Next:** Verify whether TTI has that specific role. Do not treat every 1099 opening as owner-operator or lease purchase.

### S036 — Asks which arrangement is financially better
**Say:** “We can compare documented compensation, costs, and benefits. Your personal tax situation needs advice from a qualified professional.”
**Next:** Compare actual offers without claiming a universal winner.

## 6. Qualification and driver history

Ask only fields required for the chosen path. The lines below are reusable branches, not an invented replacement for the 14-question form. Carrier eligibility is determined against current documented criteria and authorized review.

### S037 — Location or hiring area unclear
**Say:** “What ZIP should I use to check the hiring area?”
**Next:** Check eligibility and terminal access against the actual opening.

### S038 — Recently moved
**Say:** “Which location will you be working from when you start?”
**Next:** Distinguish current address from intended operating location; record accurately.

### S039 — Experience answer is vague
**Say:** “How much recent Class A driving experience do you have, and what kind of work was it?”
**Next:** Clarify dates and equipment if required; do not round experience up.

### S040 — New CDL / no experience
**Say:** “Congratulations on getting your CDL. Let me check whether we have an opening that accepts new drivers.”
**Next:** Verify a training or entry-level route; do not promise an experienced-driver role.

### S041 — Insufficient experience for selected company
**Say:** “This opening requires [verified requirement]. Let me check whether another opening fits your experience.”
**Next:** Offer only a verified alternative or explain that none is currently available.

### S042 — Experience outside the carrier's recent window
**Say:** “Let’s check the dates against this company’s current requirement.”
**Next:** Verify acceptance or any refresher process; avoid assuming past experience automatically qualifies.

### S043 — CDL class or status unclear
**Say:** “What class is your CDL, and is it currently active?”
**Next:** Use the carrier's verification process; do not infer status from an old record.

### S044 — Endorsement or restriction issue
**Say:** “Which endorsements or restrictions are on your license?”
**Next:** Check against the actual equipment and freight requirements.

### S045 — Medical certification status needs review
**Say:** “Is your required certification current, or does it need updating?”
**Next:** Route documentation/status questions through the authorized process; do not request a diagnosis or promise clearance.

### S046 — Driver reports an accident
**Say:** “Thanks for telling me. What date was it, and what does the record show?”
**Next:** Collect required factual details and route to review. Do not label it disqualifying or harmless without verification.

### S047 — Tickets or moving violations
**Say:** “Let’s get the dates and details right so the company can review accurately.”
**Next:** Apply current carrier criteria; never advise concealing a violation.

### S048 — Suspension or DUI history
**Say:** “That needs a review against the company’s current criteria. I’ll get the required facts and confirm the next step.”
**Next:** Escalate through authorized screening; do not invent an eligibility period.

### S049 — Drug/alcohol screening or Clearinghouse concern
**Say:** “That needs to go through the proper screening process. I can explain the next step, but I can’t promise clearance.”
**Next:** Refer to authorized safety/compliance review; do not suggest bypasses.

### S050 — Criminal history question
**Say:** “The company reviews that through its hiring process. Let’s use the proper review rather than guess an outcome.”
**Next:** Collect only authorized required information and escalate.

### S051 — Employment gap
**Say:** “What dates should we record for that gap, and how should it be accurately described?”
**Next:** Record the factual explanation required by the application; do not invent an employer.

### S052 — Many short jobs
**Say:** “What led to the recent moves, and what would make your next company a place you stay?”
**Next:** Record required history and identify retention needs without judgment.

### S053 — Fired from previous company
**Say:** “What happened, and what will the employer’s record show?”
**Next:** Record facts and route any eligibility uncertainty for review.

### S054 — Driver contradicts an earlier answer
**Say:** “I have [earlier answer] recorded, and you just mentioned [new answer]. Which should we use?”
**Next:** Correct the record without accusing them.

### S055 — Driver refuses a required question
**Say:** “This detail is needed for [specific purpose]. If you’d rather not provide it now, we can pause here.”
**Next:** Explain the consequence; do not invent an answer or bypass validation.

### S056 — Missing dates or documents
**Say:** “No problem. Let’s mark what’s missing and agree when you can get it.”
**Next:** Use the approved save/resume process; do not mark the application complete.

### S057 — Driver asks if they qualify before review
**Say:** “Based on what you’ve shared, this looks worth checking. Final eligibility still needs the company’s review.”
**Next:** Distinguish preliminary fit from approval.

### S058 — Driver asks you to hide or alter information
**Say:** “We need the application to be accurate. I can help explain the situation, but I can’t change or leave out required facts.”
**Next:** Keep the record accurate and escalate if necessary.

## 7. Pay, deductions, and benefits

### S059 — “Just tell me the pay.”
**Say:** “For [Company], it’s [verified amount and basis]. [Key condition]. Does that meet the range you’re looking for?”
**Next:** If eligibility/location is not yet known, explain that the opening still needs to be checked for fit.

### S060 — Wants exact weekly earnings
**Say:** “The documented pay is [basis]. Weekly earnings depend on [verified factors]. I can show you the assumptions behind the quoted range.”
**Next:** Separate documented guarantee, range, and illustration.

### S061 — Asks whether pay is guaranteed
**Say:** “The written terms say [verified guarantee and conditions],” or “I don’t have a documented guarantee for that.”
**Next:** Do not transform advertised earning potential into guaranteed income.

### S062 — Gross versus take-home
**Say:** “That figure is [verified gross/net description]. Let’s go through the documented deductions before you compare it.”
**Next:** Do not estimate personal take-home without the needed verified information.

### S063 — CPM and paid-mile method
**Say:** “The rate is [verified CPM], using [documented paid-mile method]. I’ll confirm any exclusions before you decide.”
**Next:** Explain practical terms accurately; do not assume all miles are paid identically.

### S064 — Percentage pay
**Say:** “It’s [verified percentage] of [documented revenue basis]. Let’s check what’s included and what comes out.”
**Next:** Verify freight/revenue basis, accessorials, and deductions.

### S065 — “Another company pays more.”
**Say:** “Let’s compare the same things: pay basis, workload, home time, and costs. What terms did they give you?”
**Next:** Compare documented terms; acknowledge when the other offer better meets their needs.

### S066 — Wants higher pay than available
**Say:** “I don’t have a verified opening at that level right now. Is that a firm minimum, or would [specific verified benefit] change the picture?”
**Next:** Respect the minimum if firm; do not fabricate a higher rate.

### S067 — Detention, layover, breakdown, or stop pay
**Say:** “Let me confirm the rate and the conditions for [pay type]. I want you to have the actual policy.”
**Next:** Verify trigger, amount, exclusions, and payment process.

### S068 — Sign-on bonus
**Say:** “The documented bonus is [amount], paid [schedule], subject to [conditions].”
**Next:** Verify eligibility and any repayment terms before quoting.

### S069 — Escrow, deposits, or deductions
**Say:** “Let’s go through each documented deduction and the release terms so you know what to expect.”
**Next:** If details are unavailable, get written clarification before encouraging a decision.

### S070 — Benefits, insurance, PTO, or retirement
**Say:** “For this opening, the documented benefits are [details], with [eligibility/waiting period]. What benefit matters most to you?”
**Next:** Verify the actual plan; do not imply benefits exist because the opening is W-2.

### S071 — Orientation pay or travel reimbursement
**Say:** “Let me verify what is paid, what is reimbursed, and when it is paid.”
**Next:** Confirm conditions and booking responsibilities.

### S072 — Asks if TTI charges the driver
**Say:** “I’ll confirm TTI’s current written fee policy and any separate company charges so the answer is clear.”
**Next:** State a no-fee policy only if actually verified; distinguish TTI from carrier costs.

## 8. Home time, routes, and equipment

### S073 — Needs daily home time
**Say:** “Daily home time is a must. Let me check local openings in your area.”
**Next:** If none fit, state that; do not sell OTR as local.

### S074 — Needs weekly home time
**Say:** “Which days matter, and how much time at home do you need?”
**Next:** Verify frequency and scheduling conditions, including whether particular days are assured.

### S075 — Needs a specific family date
**Say:** “What date is non-negotiable? I’ll check whether the company can accommodate it before we move forward.”
**Next:** Obtain confirmation; do not guarantee an unapproved exception.

### S076 — Rejects OTR
**Say:** “Got it. We’ll look at local or regional options that actually fit.”
**Next:** Remove incompatible options unless the driver independently changes the requirement.

### S077 — Wants dedicated work
**Say:** “Are you after a repeat route, a fixed schedule, or a specific customer account?”
**Next:** Verify what “dedicated” means for the opening.

### S078 — Refuses certain states or cities
**Say:** “Which locations are dealbreakers?”
**Next:** Check routing policy; do not promise exclusion without confirmation.

### S079 — Wants no-touch / refuses unloading
**Say:** “Is all driver unloading a dealbreaker, or are limited exceptions acceptable?”
**Next:** Verify actual freight responsibilities and exceptions.

### S080 — Wants specific trailer or freight
**Say:** “Which trailer types have you worked with, and which do you want next?”
**Next:** Match experience and preference to documented openings.

### S081 — Manual/automatic or equipment preference
**Say:** “Is that a preference or a requirement? I’ll verify the equipment for this opening.”
**Next:** Check restrictions and available truck assignment; do not guarantee a model.

### S082 — Pet or rider policy
**Say:** “Let me check the actual policy, approval process, and any costs.”
**Next:** Confirm relevant restrictions and timing.

### S083 — Parking / take truck home
**Say:** “Where would you need to park, and does the company need to approve that location?”
**Next:** Verify authorized parking and home-truck rules.

### S084 — Team driving
**Say:** “Do you already have a teammate, or are you looking to be paired?”
**Next:** Verify the team opening and each driver's qualification process.

### S085 — Camera, governor, or equipment-policy objection
**Say:** “Let’s verify [specific policy] so you can decide with the facts. Is that a firm dealbreaker for you?”
**Next:** Match or decline honestly; do not conceal the policy.

### S086 — Limited workload or schedule flexibility
**Say:** “What schedule can you realistically work?”
**Next:** Verify a compatible role rather than promising informal exceptions.

## 9. Trust and recruiter objections

### S087 — “Recruiters always lie.”

**Say:** “Fair question. Let's go through the confirmed terms and what still needs checking. I'll put those details in writing so you can compare them before deciding.”

**Next:** Use evidence, the named carrier and a clear remaining condition. Avoid personal guarantees, unverified 'best in the market' claims, invented experience, or promises to cover an income shortfall yourself.

### S088 — “Why should I trust you?”
**Say:** “Judge me by whether the details check out and whether I do what I say. What do you want to verify first?”
**Next:** Provide real verification channels and documented terms.

### S089 — Wants proof TTI is legitimate
**Say:** “You can verify us through [verified company website/contact channel]. I can send the company details before we continue.”
**Next:** Use established public contact information, not invented credentials.

### S090 — Wants the carrier's name before answering questions
**Say:** “The opening I’m discussing is with [verified Company]. I still need to confirm whether it fits your location and experience.”
**Next:** Be transparent when authorized to disclose the carrier.

### S091 — Negative reviews
**Say:** “What concern stood out? Let’s check that against the current policy and any information we can verify.”
**Next:** Acknowledge unresolved concerns; do not dismiss reviews or invent explanations.

### S092 — Bad prior experience with TTI
**Say:** “Tell me what went wrong. I’ll check the record and get the right person involved.”
**Next:** Resolve the complaint before resuming a sales pitch.

### S093 — Wants direct company contact
**Say:** “I can check the right hiring contact and arrange the next step.”
**Next:** Follow the authorized handoff process; do not obstruct verification.

### S094 — Wants written terms
**Say:** “Absolutely. I’ll send the verified details so you can review them.”
**Next:** Send a factual summary, distinguishing recruiter summary from an official offer or contract.

### S095 — Asks about your success rate
**Say:** “I don’t have a verified percentage to quote. What I can do is match your requirements and help you through the actual process.”
**Next:** Return to their decision criteria; never quote “98%” as fact.

### S096 — “Are you guaranteeing me a job?”
**Say:** “No guarantee of a hire. We can get your application to the right review process and confirm each step.”
**Next:** Explain screening and approval boundaries clearly.

## 10. Comparing and presenting companies

### S097 — One strong match
**Say:** “[Company] fits your [priority] because [verified reason]. The tradeoff is [relevant condition]. Does that work for you?”
**Next:** Confirm acceptance of the tradeoff before closing.

### S098 — Two strong matches
**Say:** “A is stronger on [priority]. B is stronger on [other priority]. Based on what you told me, I’d start with [choice]. Which works better for you?”
**Next:** Let the driver choose one for the next step.

### S099 — Driver wants every company listed
**Say:** “I can give you the broader picture. To keep it useful, let’s start with the options that meet your non-negotiables.”
**Next:** Explain shortlist criteria and share alternatives if requested.

### S100 — No company matches
**Say:** “I don’t have an opening that meets those requirements right now. Would you like me to check back if that changes?”
**Next:** Record requirements and contact preference; do not present a false match.

### S101 — Match requires compromise
**Say:** “This meets [requirement], but it falls short on [requirement]. Is that flexible, or should we rule it out?”
**Next:** Let the driver decide; do not quietly weaken their requirement.

### S102 — Opening disappears during the conversation
**Say:** “The availability needs reconfirming. Let me check before you invest more time in this option.”
**Next:** Verify status; offer an alternative with permission.

### S103 — Offer data is contradictory or old
**Say:** “I’m seeing conflicting details. I’ll verify the current terms before we move forward.”
**Next:** Pause the disputed pitch and obtain an authoritative update.

### S104 — Driver wants to apply to both finalists
**Say:** “Which would you choose if both approved you? Let’s start there and check the process for the other option.”
**Next:** In the internal matching flow, select one final company; verify any additional application process separately.

## 11. Closing objections

### S105 — Ready to apply
**Say:** “Good. Let’s get the application started. [Next required step].”
**Next:** Stop selling and help complete the agreed action.

### S106 — “I need to think.”

**Say:** “Of course. What do you still need to decide? Let's make sure you have that information, then choose a time to reconnect if you want.”

**Next:** Find the actual unresolved issue. Do not manufacture urgency or treat hesitation as agreement. For a team, bring both decision-makers into the next conversation when they choose.

### S107 — “Just send me the details.”

**Say:** “Sure. Which details do you want first: pay, home time, equipment, or the company information? I'll send the confirmed details. Would you like a follow-up call, or would you prefer to contact me?”

**Next:** Honor the actual request. Do not replace a request for information with an application link unless they separately agree. Use a specific send time you can meet, rather than 'over the next few days'.

### S108 — Wants to consult spouse/family
**Say:** “Makes sense. I’ll send the details you need. What time works after you’ve talked it over?”
**Next:** Confirm the follow-up; do not criticize family involvement.

### S109 — Waiting on another offer
**Say:** “What will decide it for you? Let’s compare the actual terms so you can choose.”
**Next:** Agree on timing without inventing scarcity.

### S110 — Current employer makes a counteroffer
**Say:** “Does it fix the reason you started looking?”
**Next:** Compare the long-term priorities; accept their choice.

### S111 — Afraid of changing jobs
**Say:** “What part feels risky—the income, the schedule, or whether the company delivers?”
**Next:** Address the named risk with verified information; do not guarantee success.

### S112 — Application feels like a commitment
**Say:** “An application starts the review. You still need to review any offer and decide whether to accept it.”
**Next:** Explain actual application terms and any separate agreements accurately.

### S113 — Wants to skip the application
**Say:** “The company needs its required application and review. I can help with the next step, but I can’t skip it.”
**Next:** Reduce friction through assistance, not bypasses.

### S114 — Hesitates but gives no reason
**Say:** “What’s holding you back?”
**Next:** Pause. Do not stack three more closes over their silence.

### S115 — Wants an unavailable exception
**Say:** “I can ask whether that exception is available. I can’t promise it until it’s approved.”
**Next:** Escalate the specific request and pause any dependent decision.

### S116 — Wants a better option later
**Say:** “What would the better option need to include? I’ll tell you whether we have it now or need to check later.”
**Next:** Record the actual threshold; arrange follow-up only if desired.

### S117 — Declines after hearing the offer
**Say:** “Understood. Is it the pay, schedule, or something else that rules it out?”
**Next:** Ask once only if they remain open to talking; otherwise thank them and end.

### S118 — Asks how long the application takes
**Say:** “It depends on the information and documents you have ready. I can help you get started and explain what’s required.”
**Next:** Quote a duration only if verified for that application; no invented “two-minute” claim.

## 12. Application and technical problems

### S119 — No text received
**Say:** “Let’s confirm the number. If you prefer, I can send it by email.”
**Next:** Verify delivery and contact details without repeatedly resending blindly.

### S120 — Link fails
**Say:** “Tell me what you see. I’ll verify the link, and we’ll use the correct one.”
**Next:** Check the actual referral/application URL; record the error if unresolved.

### S121 — Driver distrusts the link
**Say:** “You can verify the application site through our company contact channel before entering anything.”
**Next:** Give a real verification route; do not pressure them into opening it.

### S122 — No smartphone or internet
**Say:** “What device or access do you have available? Let’s check the supported way to complete it.”
**Next:** Arrange an approved alternative; do not promise an unsupported telephone application.

### S123 — Does not want to text
**Say:** “No problem. Is email better, or would you rather use another supported method?”
**Next:** Use only the agreed channel.

### S124 — Cannot open email
**Say:** “Let’s use another supported method, or schedule a time when you can access it.”
**Next:** Avoid turning the recruiting call into an endless device-support session.

### S125 — Cannot upload a document
**Say:** “What happens when you try? We can check the supported file format and upload method.”
**Next:** Use the authorized document channel; do not ask for sensitive documents through an improvised route.

### S126 — Validation error / form will not submit
**Say:** “Which field is showing the error? Let’s check the information there.”
**Next:** Record the exact error and escalate unresolved issues; never tell them to enter false data.

### S127 — Missing required information
**Say:** “Let’s save what the system allows and list what you need to finish.”
**Next:** Verify save/resume capability; do not claim unsaved data is safe.

### S128 — Says “I submitted it” but no confirmation
**Say:** “What confirmation did you see? I’ll check whether the submission reached the system.”
**Next:** Verify receipt; avoid duplicate applications until status is known.

### S129 — Duplicate or previous application
**Say:** “Let me check the existing record so we don’t create confusion. What email or number did you use?”
**Next:** Follow the approved lookup/update process and preserve history.

### S130 — Wrong company selected
**Say:** “Let’s confirm the company you intended. I’ll check how to correct it properly.”
**Next:** Use the supported correction process; do not claim a submitted application was changed without verification.

### S131 — Referral missing / wrong recruiter
**Say:** “Let me verify which recruiter the application is attached to before you submit anything else.”
**Next:** Check actual attribution. Do not generate a guessed query parameter or duplicate submission.

### S132 — Driver fears identity theft / requests privacy information
**Say:** “That’s a reasonable concern. Let’s verify the site and review its privacy information before you share anything.”
**Next:** Use official privacy/support information. Never promise blanket confidentiality or invent retention rules.

### S133 — Asks recruiter to enter sensitive information
**Say:** “Use the approved application channel for that information. I can guide you through the steps.”
**Next:** Follow authorized handling procedures; do not place sensitive values in ordinary recruiter notes.

### S134 — Application site or internal system is down
**Say:** “The system isn’t working right now. I’ll record the issue and agree a time to reconnect once we have a working path.”
**Next:** Escalate the failure; do not pretend an application was created or submitted.

## 13. Follow-up and hiring status

### S135 — Application started but incomplete
**Say:** “Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI. You started the application for [Company]. What stopped you from finishing?”
**Next:** Use only if the record confirms a start; solve the blocker rather than assuming laziness.

### S136 — Application received
**Say:** “Your application is showing as received. Next is [verified step]. I’ll update you by [time I can meet].”
**Next:** Record the next action and owner; do not imply approval.

### S137 — Review taking longer than expected
**Say:** “I don’t have a final decision yet. I’m checking [specific step], and I’ll update you by [time], even if it’s still pending.”
**Next:** Follow through and escalate delays appropriately.

### S138 — Driver demands a guaranteed start date
**Say:** “I can give you a confirmed date once the required approval and scheduling are complete.”
**Next:** Explain the actual outstanding step.

### S139 — Approved but orientation not confirmed
**Say:** “Your status is [verified approval status]. Let’s confirm orientation and travel before you make arrangements.”
**Next:** Do not let an unconfirmed tentative date become a promise.

### S140 — Orientation scheduled
**Say:** “You’re confirmed for [date/time/location]. Let’s check travel, what you need to bring, and who to call if plans change.”
**Next:** Send verified logistics and confirm understanding.

### S141 — Missed orientation
**Say:** “We missed you at orientation. Are you still interested, and what happened?”
**Next:** Verify rescheduling availability and resolve the actual blocker.

### S142 — Not approved / adverse screening outcome
**Say:** “The company is not moving forward with this application. I can connect you with the appropriate contact for the review and any required information.”
**Next:** Follow the authorized notification process. Do not invent a reason, replace formal notices, or disclose restricted screening data.

### S143 — Wants another company after rejection
**Say:** “Let’s check whether another company’s current criteria fit. I can’t assume a different outcome, but I can verify the options.”
**Next:** Do not conceal the underlying required history.

### S144 — Driver accepted another job
**Say:** “Congratulations. I’ll close out our current follow-up. If you want help in the future, you know where to reach me.”
**Next:** Update outcome and respect contact preference.

### S145 — Driver ghosts after agreement
**Say:** “Hey [Name], checking whether you still want to move forward with [Company]. If your plans changed, just let me know.”
**Next:** Use the approved contact cadence and channel; do not contact indefinitely.

### S146 — Wants to quit current job before approval
**Say:** “First, let’s confirm the offer, required approvals, and start details so you can make an informed decision.”
**Next:** Clearly state what remains unconfirmed; do not pressure them to resign.

## 14. Voicemail, text, and email

Templates are drafts, not authorization to send. Use verified identity and callback details. Text only through the approved workflow and the driver's permitted channel. Do not claim prior interest or a prior conversation unless recorded.

### S147 — First voicemail, approximately 20 seconds

**Say:** “Hi [Name], this is [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting. I'm calling about CDL-A driving opportunities. If you'd like to compare options on pay, home time or equipment, call me at [verified callback number]. Again, [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting, [verified callback number]. Have a good day.”

**Next:** If the name is missing, omit it. Use only the verified callback number for that recruiter. If the number is missing, omit the number sentences and flag configuration; do not invent one. Do not mark left unless you actually left this message.

### S148 — Follow-up voicemail after a real conversation

“Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI. Following up on [Company] like we discussed. Call me at [callback number], and we’ll confirm your next step. Again, [callback number].”

**Next:** Use the agreed follow-up timing and accurate company status.

### S149 — Voicemail about an agreed callback

“Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI. Calling at the time we agreed. Reach me at [callback number], and we’ll pick up where we left off.”

**Next:** Record the missed callback; use an approved alternative channel if appropriate.

### S150 — Full mailbox / voicemail unavailable

**Say:** No message to leave.
**Next:** Record voicemail unavailable. Use another permitted channel if available; do not redial repeatedly in the same sitting.

### S151 — Short text after missed call, when permitted

“Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting. I called about CDL opportunities. Are you looking for W-2 or 1099? You can reach me at [callback number].”

**Next:** Apply the approved messaging requirements and any opt-out handling.

### S152 — Texting an agreed application link

“[Name], here’s the application link we discussed: [verified referral link]. This is [Recruiter] with TTI Recruiting. Let me know if you need help with the next step.”

**Next:** Verify the correct path and recruiter attribution before sending.

### S153 — Incomplete-application text

“Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI. Checking whether you still want to finish the application for [Company]. Is there anything blocking you?”

**Next:** Use only with a verified incomplete record and permitted channel.

### S154 — Last follow-up in the approved sequence

“Hey [Name], [Recruiter] with TTI. I’ll leave this here for now. If you want to revisit CDL opportunities, reach me at [callback number].”

**Next:** End the current outreach sequence. Do not send this after a stop-contact request.

### S155 — Driver replies “STOP” or equivalent

**Say:** Follow the approved opt-out handling; no additional pitch.
**Next:** Suppress further outreach according to the contact workflow.

### S156 — Short email with verified offer summary

**Subject:** [Company] — the CDL option we discussed

Hi [Name],

Here are the details we discussed:

- Company: [Company]
- Employment type: [W-2/1099]
- Pay: [verified amount/basis and key conditions]
- Home time: [verified schedule/conditions]
- Routes/freight: [verified details]
- Relevant costs or deductions: [verified details]
- Next step: [application/review step]

[Verified application link, if agreed]

Call me at [callback number] if you want to go through it together.

[Recruiter]
TTI Recruiting
[Verified contact email]

**Next:** Include the required company messaging elements through the approved sending workflow. This summary is not an official employment offer.

## 15. Difficult conversations and special circumstances

### S157 — Angry or insulting driver
**Say:** “I’m happy to help if we can keep the conversation respectful. Do you want to continue?”
**Next:** If abuse continues, end calmly and record the outcome.

### S158 — Threats or immediate danger
**Say:** “I’m ending this recruiting conversation.”
**Next:** Follow the appropriate incident process. If someone reports immediate danger, direct them to appropriate local emergency assistance; do not continue selling.

### S159 — Driver is distressed about financial hardship
**Say:** “I’m sorry you’re dealing with that. I’ll be clear about the actual steps and timing so you know what to expect.”
**Next:** Avoid exploiting urgency or promising immediate income.

### S160 — Driver wants to discuss personal medical details
**Say:** “You don’t need to explain your medical history to me. Let’s use the appropriate process for any job requirements or accommodation questions.”
**Next:** Route to the authorized contact; record only necessary, authorized information.

### S161 — Accommodation request
**Say:** “I can connect you with the right person to discuss what the role requires and your request.”
**Next:** Do not independently approve, deny, or speculate about eligibility.

### S162 — Work-authorization question
**Say:** “That needs to go through the employer’s approved verification process. I can connect you with the right contact.”
**Next:** Avoid legal conclusions or assumptions based on accent, name, or nationality.

### S163 — Asks about age or another personal characteristic
**Say:** “Let’s focus on the documented requirements for the job and the employer’s review process.”
**Next:** Do not create informal screening rules or make unsupported eligibility judgments.

### S164 — Wants unsafe or improper operating practices
**Say:** “I can help you find a legitimate role, but I can’t arrange work around unsafe or improper practices.”
**Next:** Do not promise hours, routes, or instructions that bypass safety processes.

### S165 — Long story / conversation drifting
**Say:** “I hear you. To make this useful, what’s the main thing you want your next company to change?”
**Next:** Redirect respectfully to one decision-relevant point.

### S166 — Driver is skeptical about remote recruiter location
**Say:** “I’m with TTI Recruiting. What matters is that we verify the actual company terms and get you to the right hiring process. What would you like to check?”
**Next:** Answer identity/location questions honestly; do not pretend to be in the US if you are not.

### S167 — Driver asks for the recruiter's real name
**Say:** “I use [professional alias] for recruiting. My name is [real name].”
**Next:** Follow the established truthful identity policy; do not impersonate another person.

### S168 — Driver complains about repeated calls from different recruiters
**Say:** “Thanks for telling me. I’ll check who owns the contact so we can avoid overlapping calls.”
**Next:** Resolve ownership and contact preference; do not compete by spamming the driver.

## 16. Confidence lines and phrases to replace

Use a confidence line only when it naturally fits. One is enough; a call should not sound like a speech.

### Original confidence lines

- “Tell me what matters. I’ll tell you what actually fits.”
- “Let’s get you the facts and make a decision.”
- “I’m confident in this match because it lines up with what you told me.”
- “Judge me by whether the details check out and whether I follow through.”
- “You know what you want. Let’s see which company can deliver it.”
- “If the fit is there, let’s get the next step moving.”
- “I’ll handle my part. Let’s make sure your next step is clear.”

### Replace these phrases

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| “I close 98% of drivers.” | “I’ll help you check the fit and move through the process.” |
| “I guarantee you this job.” | “Final approval comes through the company’s review.” |
| “You’ll definitely make [amount].” | “The documented pay is [basis], subject to [conditions].” |
| “This is the last seat!” when unverified | “Let me confirm current availability.” |
| “Trust me.” | “Let’s verify the details.” |
| “Don’t worry about the deductions.” | “Let’s go through the documented deductions.” |
| “Sign now; read it later.” | “Review the terms before deciding.” |
| “You applied to us.” when unverified | “The source on your record is [verified source].” |
| “Just put anything in that field.” | “Let’s get the correct information or pause.” |
| “Why won’t you give me a chance?” | “What do you need clarified?” |
| “I already sent your application.” when unverified | “I’ll check whether the submission was received.” |
| “Everyone qualifies.” | “Let’s check the current criteria.” |

The desired movie-style energy is decisiveness and presence. Claims about exceptionally few failures require real evidence; use the original lines above instead of fabricated bragging.

## 17. Call outcomes and recruiter notes

Finish every call with an accurate outcome and, where needed, one owned next step.

| Outcome | Record | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| No answer | Attempt time, voicemail status | Approved follow-up sequence |
| Wrong number | Verified wrong-number outcome | Remove from active outreach |
| Stop contact | Exact contact preference | Apply suppression workflow |
| Callback agreed | Date, time, timezone, channel | Call at agreed time |
| W-2 routed | W-2 preference, link sent status | External application process |
| 1099 qualification in progress | Accurate completed answers, missing items | Resume approved sequence |
| Company chosen | Selected company and verified offer version | Correct application step |
| Application started | Verified start status, blocker | Help resolve blocker |
| Application submitted | Verified receipt and next review step | Follow status |
| Pending verification | Exact unresolved detail and owner | Verify before dependent promise |
| Declined | Reason if volunteered, contact preference | Close current opportunity |
| Handoff required | Issue, authorized recipient/team, due time | Confirm handoff occurred |

### Compact note template

```text
Contact:
Recruiter:
Employment preference: W-2 / 1099 / undecided
Driver's main priority:
Non-negotiables:
Qualification status for chosen path:
Company chosen:
Verified terms discussed:
Unresolved detail or objection:
Application status: not started / started / received / unknown
Contact permissions/preferences:
Outcome:
Next action:
Owner:
Due date/time/timezone:
```

Preserve factual history. A contact disappearing from the available calling list does not mean deleting their database record. Do not put sensitive identification or unnecessary personal information in free-text notes.

## 18. Universal fallback and combined scenarios

### S169 — Unexpected question with no prepared answer

**Say:** “I don't have a confirmed answer to that yet. I'll check [specific question] with [Company] and come back to you at [agreed time]. Does that need to be settled before you move forward?”

**Next:** Preserve the driver's place in the call. Record the exact question and whether it blocks the decision. Return from the FAQ to the previous call step. If the driver has not agreed to move forward, do not mark an application.

### S170 — Multiple objections at once
**Say:** “Let’s take them one at a time. Which one decides whether this works for you?”
**Next:** Resolve the main blocker first. Do not answer a stack of objections with a longer pitch.

### S171 — Recruiter makes a mistake
**Say:** “I need to correct something I told you: [correction]. The verified detail is [fact]. Does that change your decision?”
**Next:** Correct the record and any dependent next step immediately.

### S172 — You cannot fulfill a promised callback time
**Say:** “I said I’d update you by [time]. The answer is still pending. Here’s what I know, and here’s the next update I can commit to.”
**Next:** Send an update through the agreed channel instead of disappearing.

### Mini-dialogue A — Busy driver, pay question, W-2

**Driver:** “I’m busy. What’s the pay?”

**Recruiter:** “For [verified opening], it’s [amount/basis and key condition]. Are you looking for W-2 or 1099?”

**Driver:** “W-2.”

**Recruiter:** “Got it. We use the W-2 application site. Would you like the link, or a callback when you’ve got time?”

**Driver:** “Send it.”

**Recruiter:** “Can I text this number?”

**Action:** Send the verified W-2 referral link through the approved workflow. Do not start the internal 1099 questionnaire.

### Mini-dialogue B — Skeptical 1099 driver, deductions concern

**Driver:** “Recruiters always promise big money, then the deductions eat it.”

**Recruiter:** “Fair concern. Let’s check the actual pay basis and deductions. What weekly range would make the move worthwhile?”

**Driver:** “[Target].”

**Recruiter:** “Got it. I’ll compare the documented terms against that. First, let’s confirm the qualification details for the 1099 options.”

**Action:** Complete required qualification, verify compensation and costs, then present a genuine match. If the earnings target cannot be substantiated, say so.

### Mini-dialogue C — Home-time requirement, two companies

**Driver:** “I need to be home every weekend.”

**Recruiter:** “Which days and how much time at home are non-negotiable?”

**Driver:** “[Requirement].”

**Recruiter:** “A has [verified schedule]. B has [verified schedule]. A lines up better with what you need, subject to [condition]. Does that condition work for you?”

**Driver:** “Yes.”

**Recruiter:** “Good. Want to start the application for A?”

**Action:** Select one company, confirm terms, and use the correct application path.

### Mini-dialogue D — Interested but uncertain qualification

**Driver:** “I want it, but I had an accident last year. Am I out?”

**Recruiter:** “Let’s get the date and facts right and have the company review them. I don’t want to guess either way.”

**Action:** Complete the required factual review; do not promise eligibility or reject on an invented rule.

### Mini-dialogue E — Hesitation after a good match

**Driver:** “Sounds good. Let me think.”

**Recruiter:** “Of course. What do you need to feel clear about?”

**Driver:** “Whether the home time is real.”

**Recruiter:** “Let’s verify the written schedule and its conditions first. Then you can decide.”

**Action:** Resolve the actual uncertainty. Close only after the driver has the information they need.

### Mini-dialogue F — No honest match

**Driver:** “Daily home time, [pay minimum], and no weekends.”

**Recruiter:** “Those are your requirements. I don’t have a verified opening that meets all three right now. Would you like me to check back if one becomes available?”

**Action:** Record the criteria and permitted follow-up. A truthful no-match is better than an application to a job the driver will reject.
